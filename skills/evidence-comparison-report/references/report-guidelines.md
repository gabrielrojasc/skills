# Report guidelines

## Evidence strength

Prefer evidence in this order:

1. Primary sources such as official specifications, policies, pricing, changelogs, source code, and scientific papers.
2. Independent tests with disclosed methods.
3. Real-world reports from owners, users, forums, and long-term reviews.
4. Counterevidence such as known failures, complaints, and findings that challenge the leading recommendation.

Treat search snippets, affiliate roundups, marketing claims, and uncited assertions as weak evidence.

Use confidence labels only when they help the reader:

- **High** means direct primary evidence or agreement among strong independent sources.
- **Medium** means credible but limited, candidate-specific, or partly indirect evidence.
- **Low** means anecdotal, weakly sourced, outdated, or subjective evidence.

## Bias checks

Check whether:

- User examples have narrowed discovery without justification.
- Commercial incentives distort a source.
- Newer or more popular candidates receive unsupported preference.
- Specifications substitute for observed behavior.
- Precise scores imply evidence that does not exist.
- Research volume is being mistaken for complete coverage.

Run a counterevidence pass proportional to the decision's cost and uncertainty.

## Report structure

Include only the sections the reader needs:

- Recommendation, ranking, shortlist, or benchmark finding.
- Candidate scope and hard exclusions.
- Evaluation criteria.
- Evidence-backed comparison.
- Important tradeoffs and best-for guidance.
- Confidence, uncertainty, and stale-risk notes.
- Sources and a short methodology note.

For HTML, make the report self-contained and default to dark mode. Use visible links, compact tables, restrained styling, and readable contrast. For Markdown, keep tables narrow enough to review as source text.
